The ritual and its missing half

Everyone predicts. Almost nobody grades.

Every November to January, people who think for a living write down what next year will bring. Then the year happens and the lists are forgotten.

The few who grade themselves grade generously, because the bar was never written down.

One list, two honest grades

Mike Ippolito, 2025 predictions

Graded by himself: 21 of 27 (78%)

Graded by a trade publication: 48%

Neither grader wrote down the bar.

The idea

Three bars, written before any evidence

Strict
what a hostile critic would require: the literal claim, numbers and dates intact
Standard
what a fair reader would accept as "this happened"
Lenient
the weakest reading a critic would still concede counts
  • Lenient is lenient about the claim, never about the evidence. Social chatter meets no bar.
  • An author admitting a miss is strong evidence. A victory lap is weak.
  • The gap between lenient and strict is the number: how much the grade depends on who is grading.

Caveats

What this is not

  • Run on October 24 with ten weeks left. Everything with a December horizon is provisional; the midterm claims are open.
  • Text only: both the reasoning model and the classifier. A few claims hinge on things a screenshot would settle.
  • Our bars are ours. The review sheets are public and the runs replay; argue with the bars, and the regrade is one command.
  • Guest predictions on a subject's podcast are shown apart from the subject's own.
The ask

Before you publish your 2027 list

If you are about to publish

Write your three bars now, while you still mean them, and publish them with the list.

If you published a 2026 list

Run it on yourself and post the receipts. The tool drafts the thread.
