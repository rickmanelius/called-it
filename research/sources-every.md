## Example predictions (verbatim where available)

**#1 Every's 2026 Predictions**
- Shipper: "Anything a user can do in an app, an agent can also do."
- Shipper, on visual creators who could not code: "Now they're going to be able to code."
- Kate Lee: most companies will have "someone whose job is to continuously optimize how the organization uses AI."
- Quintero: "AI will allow tech-forward firms to synthesize an even broader amount of information."
- Willie Williams (wildcard): "A mass-market robotic pet succeeds by the end of 2026."

**#2 Four Predictions (section headings as written; transcript needed for the wording)**
- "#1 The next generation of software will treat agents like first-class citizens"
- "#2 Designers will start building their own tools"
- "#3 The new kind of software engineer will direct AI agents"
- "#4 The next wave of AI training will index for independence"
- Gell (paraphrase): Cursor-like tools must hide the code layer to reach designers at scale.

**#3 Hoffman episode, Shipper's own words (transcript)**
- "the most addictive technology of 2026, the narrative we might be talking about at the end of the year, is how addictive it is to just make things."
- "I think OpenAI is currently missing the real coding market."
- Most likely to stumble: "I would guess Cursor. That's probably the highest likelihood."
- "It's so clear to me that agents are going to reshape how we think about company operations."
- Hoffman: "I think next year is going to be much more negative on AI than this year."; "#2 AGI in 2026 will be one person with the capacity of a team"; "#5 Biology will be the next 'language' AI learns".

**#4 Trenchcoat (Hoffman restated)**
- "Agents will break out of coding into everything else."
- "Enterprises that don't deploy agents at every meeting will fall behind."
- "Discourse will get uglier even as the tools improve."

**Prior year (2025 piece), for ritual context only**
- Duffy: "it's more likely 2025 will see the rise of specialized agents tackling very specific, measurable problems."
- Duffy: "Physical world integrations like AI-powered robots will progress much more slowly than pure software applications."
- Sharma: "Watch this trigger the great migration back to blogs."
